## Why this lab matters

A robot that can reach a point is not necessarily useful there. Near a singularity it moves slowly in one direction, needs huge joint speeds to follow a commanded path, and its force capability becomes very uneven (MR §5.3–5.4). For an assistive arm that touches a person these are safety properties, so designers check them **over the whole task**, not at one pose. Today:

* **Biomedical — table-top rehabilitation / assistive arm.** The planar robot `rehab_2r` ($L_1 = L_2 = 0.35$ m, horizontal plane) guides a patient's hand to 25 therapy targets on a table (`therapy_targets.csv`, a 0.40 × 0.20 m grid in front of the patient). You map its manipulability over the table, then **redesign** it: choose the link ratio and where to clamp its base to the table edge.
* **Biomedical — wheelchair-mounted feeding arm.** The SRS 7R arm of project scenario S2 (`srs_7r_feeding`) brings a spoon from the plate to the user's mouth (`feeding_path.csv`, synthetic). You track $\mu_1$ and $\mu_3$ along the path and find where it becomes poorly conditioned.
* **Defence — statics and singularities of EOD and load-carriage systems.** You check your statics on the exoskeleton and load cases of Workshop 10, and classify the singularities of the UGV-mounted EOD arm (`eod_6r_ugv`, scenario S4, Workshop P3).
* **The course arm.** A joint-space singularity map of the course arm tells you which hardware poses to avoid in the project sessions H1/H2.

All robot numbers are those of Course resources: *Course robot models and hardware safety checklist* (copied into your Lab code folder as `data/robots.json`); geometry and loads are illustrative unless they are textbook values.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the lab you can:

1. compute joint torques from end-effector wrenches with $\tau = J^{\mathsf T}\mathcal F$ in either frame, convert wrenches between frames, and find the wrenches a robot resists without torque, $\mathrm{Null}(J^{\mathsf T})$ (MR Eq. 5.26, p. 189);
2. compute manipulability and force ellipsoids and the measures $\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3$, and explain why the linear and angular parts are treated separately (MR Eq. 5.27, pp. 196–197);
3. detect and classify singularities from the rank and singular values of $J$, and interpret lost twists and null motions (MR §5.3);
4. map a measure over a task region, and use worst-case manipulability to choose link lengths and a base location (workspace design);
5. turn these results into concrete design rules for your project proposal.

# Procedure and checkpoints

### Checkpoint 1 — Statics (≈ 10 min)

1. `joint_torques(J, F)` $= J^{\mathsf T}\mathcal F$ (MR Eq. 5.26) and `wrench_from_torques(J, tau)` $= J^{-\mathsf T}\tau$, raising `ValueError` if $J$ is not square or is singular (MR p. 188).
2. `body_to_space_wrench(F_b, T_sb)` $= [\mathrm{Ad}_{T_{bs}}]^{\mathsf T}\mathcal F_b$ (MR Eq. 3.98) and `holding_wrench_space(m, p, g)` $= (p\times f, f)$ with $f = -m\mathbf g$.
3. `wrench_null_space(J)`: the last $6 - \operatorname{rank}$ left singular vectors of $J$.

**Checkpoint values.** Exoskeleton `arm_exo_2r` holding 2 kg (gravity $-\hat y$): $\tau = (5.886, 0)$ N m at $(0, 90^\circ)$, $(12.753, 6.867)$ at $(0, 0)$, $(0, 0)$ at $(90^\circ, 0)$ (Workshop P1). At $(0, 90^\circ)$, $\tau = (5, 2)$ N m gives $f_\text{tip} = (-5.714, 10.0)$ N. Course arm at $q_\text{home}$ holding the 0.25 kg maximum payload at the flange: $\tau = (0, -0.574, -0.351, -0.187, 0.000, 0)$ N m — the same as the difference of the gravity torques of `course_arm(payload=0.25)` and `course_arm()` in the shared dynamic model. Haptic paddle: $\dim\mathrm{Null}(J_b^{\mathsf T}) = 5$ (MR's door, p. 189); course arm with $\theta_3 = 0$: 1.
Tests: `test_joint_torques_*`, `test_space_and_body_torques_agree`, `test_power_balance`, `test_holding_wrench_*`, `test_exoskeleton_load_torques`, `test_wrench_*`.

### Checkpoint 2 — Ellipsoids and measures (≈ 10 min)

1. `manipulability_ellipsoid(J)`: eigen-decomposition of $A = JJ^{\mathsf T}$, semi-axes $\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ sorted longest first (MR Eq. 5.27).
2. `force_ellipsoid(J)`: same directions, semi-axes $1/\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ (`inf` at a singularity) (MR p. 197).
3. `manipulability_measures(J)` → `dict(mu1, mu2, mu3)` (MR pp. 196–197).

**Checkpoint values.** MR's 2R ($L_1 = L_2 = 1$): semi-axes $(2.073, 0.341)$ at $(0, \pi/4)$ and $(1.073, 0.659)$ at $(0, 3\pi/4)$ (MR p. 171, Fig. 5.3). `rehab_2r` at $(0, 90^\circ)$: $\mu_1 = 2.618$, $\mu_2 = 6.854$, $\mu_3 = 0.1225$ (Workshop P2). For any posture, $\mu_3(A)\,\mu_3(B) = 1$. The **angular** measures of $J_s$ and $J_b$ agree; the **linear** ones do not (MR p. 196: use $J_v$ of $J_b$ for the tool point).
Tests: `test_ellipsoid_*`, `test_force_ellipsoid_*`, `test_measures_*`, `test_volume_product_constant`, `test_angular_measures_frame_independent_linear_not`.

### Checkpoint 3 — Singularities (≈ 5 min)

`singularity_report(J)` → `dict(rank, sigma_min, lost_twists, null_motions)` from the SVD $J = U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}$.

**Checkpoint values.** EOD arm (Workshop P3): rank 4 at $\theta = 0$ (Cases I + II), 5 at $(0, -30^\circ, 60^\circ, 0, 0, 0)$ (Case I, null motion $\propto(0,0,0,1,0,-1)$), 6 at $(0, -30^\circ, 60^\circ, 0, 45^\circ, 0)$ with $\sigma_\text{min}(J_s) = 0.299$, and 5 at $(0, -45^\circ, -107.57^\circ, 0, 45^\circ, 0)$ (Case III, wrist centre on the turret axis). $\operatorname{rank}J_s = \operatorname{rank}J_b$ everywhere, and the needle guide (`desktop_6r`) has the same singularities as the bare course arm (MR Fig. 5.10).
Tests: `test_rank_space_equals_body`, `test_eod_singularity_cases`, `test_lost_twists_and_null_motions`, `test_singularity_independent_of_tool_frame`.

### Checkpoint 4 — Manipulability maps and workspace design of the rehabilitation arm (≈ 15 min)

1. `manipulability_map_2r(L1, L2, xs, ys, measure, elbow, limits, base)`: for every table point, solve `ik_2r_given` relative to the base, reject points out of reach or outside the joint limits (NaN), evaluate the measure of `jacobian_2r`.
2. `region_score(...)` → `(worst, fraction)`: worst case over the 25 targets (minimum $\mu_3$, or maximum $\mu_1$) and the fraction of targets reachable within limits.
3. `design_search(total, ratios, base_offsets, points, limits, measure)`: $L_1 = \rho\cdot0.70$, $L_2 = 0.70 - L_1$, base clamped at $(0, -b)$ on the table edge, $\rho = 0.30, 0.35, \dots, 0.70$, $b = 0, 0.05, \dots, 0.20$ m; best = feasible design (all 25 targets) with the best worst case.

**Checkpoint values** (`rehab_2r` limits $\theta_1\in[0, \pi]$, $\theta_2\in[0.2, 2.8]$ rad, elbow $+1$). Over a 1.4 × 1.4 m grid the largest $\mu_3$ is $0.1225 = L_1L_2$ on the circle $r = 0.35\sqrt2 = 0.495$ m (θ₂ = 90°) and the smallest $\mu_1$ is $1.618$ (θ₂ ≈ 132°) — an equal-link arm is never isotropic. Current design with the base at the table edge ($b = 0$): only 92 % of the targets reachable (the two near-right targets need $\theta_1 < 0$). With $b = 0.10$ m: all targets, worst $\mu_3 = 0.1061$, worst $\mu_1 = 3.880$.
Design search, worst-$\mu_3$ criterion: **$\rho = 0.5$ (0.35/0.35 m), $b = 0.10$ m, worst $\mu_3 = 0.1061$**. Worst-$\mu_1$ criterion: **$\rho = 0.6$ (0.42/0.28 m), $b = 0$, worst $\mu_1 = 2.297$** ($L_1/L_2 = 1.5$, close to the isotropic ratio $\sqrt2$ of Practice P1d). The two criteria choose different robots — your memo must say which one the therapy task needs, and why.
Tests: `test_map_*`, `test_region_score_values`, `test_design_search`.

### Checkpoint 5 — Spatial arms: feeding path and course-arm map (≈ 10 min in class, finish at home)

1. `measures_along_path(Slist, M, Q, rows)`: $\mu_1$, $\mu_3$ and $\sigma_\text{min}$ of the selected rows of $J_b$ for every sample of a joint path.
2. `joint_space_map(Slist, M, q0, i, j, gi, gj, measure, rows)`: a measure of $J_b$ over a grid of joints $i, j$ (0-based) with the others at $q_0$.

**Checkpoint values.** Feeding arm, linear rows $J_v$ (spoon-tip velocity; the arm has 7 joints, so $J_v$ is $3\times7$): $\mu_3$ = 0.1358 at the plate, peak 0.1426 at $t = 1.35$ s, **0.0149 at the mouth pose** (the minimum, last sample); $\mu_1$ rises from 2.95 to **57.6** and first exceeds 10 at $t = 3.15$ s (elbow $\theta_4 = -33.5^\circ$); 37 % of the path has $\mu_1 > 10$. Course arm, $\sigma_\text{min}$ of the full $J_b$ over $\theta_3, \theta_5\in[-165^\circ, 165^\circ]$ (5° grid, other joints at $q_\text{home}$): zero on the lines $\theta_3 = 0$ (elbow, Case II) and $\theta_5 = 0$ (wrist, four parallel axes), largest value 0.0675; 22.5 % of the grid has $\sigma_\text{min} < 0.01$. # Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause and fix |
|---|---|
| Torques have the wrong sign | You used the load's weight $m\mathbf g$ instead of the wrench the robot applies, $f = -m\mathbf g$; or the wrench is force-first. MR wrenches are $(m, f)$. |
| `joint_torques` differs between $J_s$ and $J_b$ | $\mathcal F$ and $J$ are in different frames; convert with `body_to_space_wrench` ($[\mathrm{Ad}_{T_{bs}}]^{\mathsf T}$, not $[\mathrm{Ad}_{T_{sb}}]$). |
| Ellipsoid semi-axes look squared | Semi-axes are $\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ of $JJ^{\mathsf T}$. Also check you did not use $J^{\mathsf T}J$ ($n\times n$, joint space). |
| `force_ellipsoid` directions in a different order from the test | Return them in the *same order* as `manipulability_ellipsoid` (longest velocity axis first), not re-sorted by force length. |
| $\mu_3$ is $10^{-8}$, not 0, at a singularity | Round-off in the eigenvalues: treat $\lambda\le10^{-12}\lambda_\text{max}$ as zero, as the docstring says. |
| `numpy.linalg.eig` gives complex numbers or unordered values | $A$ is symmetric: use `eigh` / `eigvalsh` (real, ascending order). |
| Map has NaN where the point is clearly reachable | `ik_2r_given` must get the coordinates **relative to the base**: `ik_2r_given(x - bx, y - by, ...)`; and check you pass `elbow=+1` for `rehab_2r` (θ₂ ≥ 0.2). |
| `design_search` best has fraction < 1 | Filter the feasible designs (all targets reachable) before choosing the best. |
| `measures_along_path` 10× off | You used $J_s$ (velocity of the point at the base origin) or the full $J$; the test uses the **linear rows of $J_b$**. |
| `ModuleNotFoundError: starter` | Run `pytest` from inside your Lab code folder, or set `LAB_IMPL` to your file name without `.py`. |

# Pre-lab answers (released after the session)

1. The elbow torque $\tau_2$: the load has no moment arm about the elbow axis (Workshop P1a; MR's suitcase example, p. 175).
2. Manipulability $\sqrt{\lambda_i}$, force $1/\sqrt{\lambda_i}$, along the same eigenvectors; the longest velocity axis is the shortest force axis (MR p. 197).
3. $\det(JJ^{\mathsf T}) = (\det J)^2$, so $\mu_3 = |\det J|$; for the 2R arm $\mu_3 = L_1L_2|\sin\theta_2|$, independent of $\theta_1$.
4. The rows mix rad/s and m/s (and the result depends on the unit of length); use the linear rows $J_v$ of $J_b$ for the tool point, and the angular rows separately (MR p. 196).
5. $\dim = 6 - 1 = 5$; e.g. any pure moment about $\hat x_b$, or a force along $\hat x_b$ through the pivot, $(0, 0, 0.090,\ 1, 0, 0)$ (Practice P2c).

**C++ troubleshooting.**

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `cpp_support not found` | the course folder `cpp_support` is not next to / above your Lab code folder | copy it there, or configure with `-DSBEG_CPP_SUPPORT=<path>` |
| `No CMAKE_CXX_COMPILER could be found` | no C++ compiler installed | install it (see Requirements); on Windows run from the "x64 Native Tools Command Prompt" if Ninja cannot find `cl` |
| `cannot open file: .../cpp_cases.json` or `.../robots.json` | Lab code folder moved after configuring | delete the `build` folder and configure again |
| "Eigen: wrong matrix size or index out of range" | e.g. `rightCols(6 - rank)` on a $2\times2$ planar Jacobian (use `J.rows() - rank`), or `q(i)` with a 0-based index past the end | check the sizes; the test program does not crash, the test simply fails |
| `null_motions` wrong, `lost_twists` right | used `svd.matrixV()` as if it were $V^{\mathsf T}$ (rows instead of columns) | take the last columns of `svd.matrixV()` |
| `test_design_search` picks another design with the same score | replaced the best on equal scores | keep the first one on a tie (strict `<` or `>`), as Python's `max` |
| `test_measures_singular` fails with a huge but finite $\mu_1$ | round-off eigenvalue not set to zero | treat $\lambda\le10^{-12}\max(1,\lambda_\text{max})$ as 0 before dividing |
| Results wrong only in some entries, no compile error | an Eigen product stored in `auto` (`auto A = J * J.transpose();` keeps references to temporaries) | write the type: `Eigen::MatrixXd A = J * J.transpose();` |
| Tests still show your old result | program not rebuilt | `cmake --build build` before `./build/lab_tests` |
